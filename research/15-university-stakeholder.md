# 15. The University as Stakeholder, Gatekeeper, and Eventual Customer

*Research track for a free, bottom-up "operating system" for college clubs. Compiled 2026-09-10.*

**Method note.** The session's web-search budget was exhausted before this track began, so everything below was gathered by fetching primary sources directly (university policy pages, statute summaries, regulator FAQs, trade press) — roughly 45 distinct sources. Several high-value pages (Harvard DSO, Michigan Center for Campus Involvement, EDUCAUSE, NASPA, Discord support) block automated fetches; where I could not verify a claim from a primary source I say so explicitly. One caution worth recording: an automated summary of the Texas A&M manual asserted Venmo, FERPA and "IT approval for third-party software" rules that the manual's actual text does not contain — I discarded those and used only text verified by grep against the PDF.

### 1.2 Patterns that matter for a bottom-up platform

1. **Money is where policies bite hardest, and it splits into two camps.** "Custodial" campuses (Penn State, Texas A&M, UIUC, Berkeley-sponsored orgs, Georgia Tech) require all club money to sit in a university-administered account; Penn State explicitly bans "mobile financial applications." "Independent" campuses (Ohio State, UT Austin RSOs, Berkeley-unsponsored) let clubs bank anywhere with an EIN. A payments feature must be configurable per campus, and on custodial campuses it must be *ledger + reimbursement request*, not a wallet.
2. **The portal is mandatory for the compliance surface, not for daily life.** Every large campus above routes annual registration, roster-of-record, officer/advisor designation, event approval, travel registration and waivers through Engage (HornsLink, CalLink, Get Involved, CardinalEngage, Discover). None of the handbooks reviewed forbids clubs from using other tools; they simply define what must be filed in the official system. Berkeley's CalLink page comes closest to a mandate ("at least 4 signatories on their roster at all times") — a roster-of-record rule, not a tool-exclusivity rule.
3. **Non-agency is the legal spine.** UIUC codifies that RSOs are not "units or agents of the university"; UT Austin says RSOs are "not 'officially sponsored'"; Texas A&M's whole quadrant model is a risk-and-affiliation classification. Universities want the *option* of distance from club conduct. A tool that makes clubs look more official (university marks, .edu-styled branding) triggers trademark offices; a tool that keeps the RSO/SSO line clean does not.
4. **Name and mark rules are universal and enforced.** UT Austin, UIUC and Texas A&M all regulate the university name in org names. Our product must never auto-generate "University of X Club Hub"-style branding and should carry an explicit "not affiliated with" disclaimer per campus.
5. **Advisors are a university employee role, not a club role.** Texas A&M and Georgia Tech require advisors to be full-time employees. Advisor accounts are therefore our first natural bridge into staff — and a FERPA-relevant one, since an employee viewing a roster is acting for the institution.
6. **Community colleges are unserved.** NOVA's Google Sheet is typical. The bottom-up wedge is widest there, but so is the absence of a buyer.

### 1.3 Hazing: the Stop Campus Hazing Act changes what a platform must be able to produce

The Act was signed in December 2024 ([StopHazing](https://www.stophazing.org/policy/stop-campus-hazing-act)). Per [Inside Higher Ed's July 2025 implementation report](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/safety/2025/07/01/federal-antihazing-law-takes-shape-campuses) and Georgia Tech's [public disclosure page](https://osi.gatech.edu/hazing-conduct-history):

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Clery statistics | Hazing counted from **January 1, 2025**; reported in the Annual Security Report ("The only thing that the act requires to be submitted to the federal government is the annual crime statistics" — S. Daniel Carter) |
| Campus Hazing Transparency Report | Data collection from **July 1, 2025**; first report due **December 23, 2025**; updated at least **twice yearly**; posted "on a prominent location on the institution's website"; retained **five years** |
| Content | "Every hazing policy violation incident," the specific organizations, sanctions, and enough information for prospective members to decide whether to join — including derecognized groups "still operating informally on the periphery" |
| Prevention | Campus-wide, research-based programs reaching all students, faculty and staff |
| Uniform definition | Institutions had to adopt the federal definition by July 1, 2025 |

Georgia Tech already publishes org name, incident dates, violation, alcohol/drug involvement, sanction and status, and cites Georgia's Max Gruver Act (posting within 15 days, retained five years). **Implication:** universities now need per-organization conduct histories that follow the org across officer turnover. A platform with a stable org identity, an officer/roster timeline and an exportable event log is directly useful; a platform where hazing planning could happen in unmoderated DMs is a liability they will be asked about. Texas A&M's rule that "Hazing investigations will be reported to the University Police" means our data can end up in a criminal file.

### 1.4 Title IX and DMs

The 2024 Title IX rule was vacated by a federal court in January 2025 and the Department directed schools back to the 2020 regulations ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_IX)). Under the 2020 framework a school must respond to sexual harassment in its "education program or activity," which includes contexts over which it exercises substantial control. Practical consequences:

- While we are an unofficial tool, harassment in our DMs is largely outside the university's control and therefore outside its mandated response — but students will still report it to Title IX offices, who will ask us for logs.
- Once we are official (contracted, SSO-linked), our DMs become part of the "program or activity," and Title IX offices will expect: a report/block flow, retention sufficient to investigate, and a lawful path to hand over message history. CUNY already requires org leaders to complete sexual-misconduct training before recognition; a platform that can evidence training completion is welcome.
- Vendors are not "responsible employees," but advisors who see harassment inside our tool are. Design advisor views accordingly.

### 1.5 Recognized vs unrecognized

Recognition is a bundle of privileges (space, funding, marks, the portal, advising — see [Texas A&M's RSO rights page](https://studentaffairs.tamu.edu/rso-rights/)) in exchange for a compliance surface. The unrecognized layer is large: derecognized SJP chapters at Columbia, GWU, Penn and UIUC in 2023–24 ([SJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_Justice_in_Palestine)), Harvard's final clubs, frozen orgs at UT Austin, and every informal group chat that never registered. Engage-style portals structurally cannot serve unrecognized groups, because the university is the tenant. **This is our structural advantage and our biggest political risk:** we will be the tool of record for groups the university has cut off, which is exactly what the Hazing Act's "still operating informally" language targets.

### 2.2 What gets a tool banned, tolerated, or adopted

**Banned** (or blocked) when it combines *anonymity + harm to students + no institutional lever*. Every ban above is about anonymous speech, not data. Universities acted only when a president could frame it as student welfare, and even then the block was network-level theater.

**Tolerated** when the tool is (a) identity-bound, (b) does not claim affiliation, (c) does not touch money or minors, and (d) doesn't force the university to answer for it. GroupMe, RMP and Coursicle live here. Note the FERPA logic: data students put into GroupMe is not an "education record" because it is not "maintained by an educational agency or institution or by a party acting for the agency" ([34 CFR 99.3](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa)). Unofficial tools are FERPA-invisible, which is why IT does not chase them.

**Adopted** when the tool crosses three gates:

1. **FERPA "school official" gate** — the vendor must perform "an institutional service or function for which the agency or institution would otherwise use employees," be "under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records," and accept redisclosure limits ([§99.31(a)(1)](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa); [PTAC FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/frequently-asked-questions)). "Direct control" is the clause that ends bottom-up autonomy.
2. **Security gate** — HECVAT. Version 4.0 (2025) replaced Full/Lite with one adaptive instrument: a "Core 60" (~61 questions) baseline and 250–270 questions for high-risk vendors; used by the Ivies, UC and CSU; since the Community Broker Index retired July 31, 2025 vendors host it in their own trust centers ([UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/blog/hecvat)). Expect SOC 2 Type II to be requested alongside.
3. **Accessibility gate** — DOJ's Title II web rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA for public universities' web content and apps, explicitly including third-party "calendars, scheduling tools... reservations systems, and payment systems" they deploy; the [ADA.gov page](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) now lists April 26, 2027 (entities ≥50k population) and April 26, 2028 (smaller) — a year later than the original 2024 rule's dates, so verify whether the extension is final. Either way, a VPAT will be a procurement precondition within 12 months.

The 2021 Fizz episode is the cautionary tale for us specifically: a student-built, non-anonymous-adjacent tool with a misconfigured Firestore and lawyers who threatened the students who found it. Security posture and disclosure culture are reputational assets on a campus where the student newspaper is still writing about it three years later.

## 3. The student activities office as a buyer

### 3.1 Who they are

The office is usually titled Student Activities / Student Involvement / Campus Life / Student Engagement, headed by a Director reporting to a Dean of Students, who reports to a Vice President for Student Affairs (VPSA). At Texas A&M the unit ("Student Activities," with sub-teams SOLAD and SOFC) serves 1,300+ orgs; at Georgia Tech the Center for Student Engagement reported 614 active RSOs and 3,082 events in 2024-25. Ohio State publishes a single email and phone number for the whole function. Typical staffing at a mid-size campus is a handful of professionals plus graduate assistants; program budgets are small and the software line is often owned by the VPSA or a Student Affairs IT/assessment director rather than the Director of Activities.

The macro backdrop is contraction: IHE's cost-cutting tracker recorded colleges cutting "more than 300 jobs" in February 2026 alone, and student affairs is a routine target ([IHE, March 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/business/cost-cutting/2026/03/03/february-brought-flurry-program-and-personnel-cuts)).

### 3.2 The assessment mandate

Student affairs must justify itself in retention terms. The instruments are the [CAS standards](https://www.cas.edu/standards) (50 functional areas including Campus Activities Programs, with Self-Assessment Guides "for self-study or self-assessment purposes") and the vendors' own dashboards. Encoura now markets Engage as "the market-leading co-curricular engagement platform for student affairs" next to retention surveys claiming "+8% average retention lift" and reaching "60% of at-risk students" ([Encoura Student Success](https://www.encoura.org/student-success/)). CampusGroups sells a "dynamic Co-Curricular Transcript" and "Data Intelligence" dashboards with a data export API ([Ready Education](https://www.readyeducation.com/campusgroups/)). What VPSAs report upward: co-curricular participation rate (share of enrolled students on at least one roster or with at least one event check-in), first-year involvement by term, involvement-vs-persistence cross-tabs, and hazing/conduct disclosures. **Event check-in by card swipe or QR is the atomic data point for all of it**, which is why NACCU (the campus ID association; [naccu.org](https://www.naccu.org/)) sits adjacent to this market.

### 3.3 Procurement

- **Decision makers:** VPSA or Dean of Students sponsors; Director of Student Activities is the champion; CIO/IT security signs HECVAT; General Counsel signs the FERPA/data addendum; accessibility office signs the VPAT; procurement runs the RFP if over threshold (commonly $10k–$50k at publics).
- **Timeline:** 6–18 months at publics; fiscal year starts July 1, so budget asks land in winter. Contracts are usually 3–5 years with auto-renewal.
- **Price band:** Engage/CampusGroups/Presence are not list-priced. *Estimate, not sourced:* roughly $15k–$75k per year by enrollment, with SIS integration and mobile-app add-ons on top. Handshake proved that a "small annual fee" from each school scales when students are free.
- **Incumbents:** Anthology Engage (ex-Campus Labs; the HornsLink/CalLink/Get Involved/CardinalEngage/Discover installations above), CampusGroups (Ready Education), Presence (Modern Campus; the product page moved and I could not retrieve current positioning).

### 3.4 The Anthology collapse and what it means for Engage customers

- Anthology — formed in 2020 by combining Campus Management, Campus Labs and iModules, then buying Blackboard in 2021 — filed Chapter 11 in late September 2025 with "over $1 billion in debt," having "lost nearly $80 million in revenue over the past two years." Oaktree and Nexus provided $50M; the plan was to keep only Blackboard. **Encoura was the stalking-horse bidder for the "life-cycle engagement and student success businesses"** (i.e., Engage) and Ellucian for the enterprise/SIS businesses ([IHE, Oct 2025](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/administrative-tech/2025/10/08/why-ed-tech-behemoth-unraveled); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackboard_Inc.)).
- Encoura's site dates its acquisition of the Anthology units to **February 2, 2026** and now lists Engage, Raise, Encompass, One-Stop and Help Desk under its brand, framed as "a fully connected intelligence ecosystem where enrollment and retention combine with agentic AI" ([Encoura](https://www.encoura.org/anthology/)). Anthology rebranded back to Blackboard in March 2026.
- Analyst Phil Hill's diagnosis — Anthology "misunderstood" that "academics... really pick the LMS" separately from other systems — applies in reverse to us: student affairs picks its own tools, and they are cheap enough to be replaced without a campus-wide fight.
- **Switching window:** Engage contracts signed 2021–2023 come up for renewal 2026–2028, exactly as ownership changed twice in six months and Encoura repositions Engage as an enrollment-marketing adjunct. Campuses that bought Campus Labs for student affairs are now customers of an enrollment-data company. That is an unusually open door, and Encoura's [Engage marketing page](https://www.encoura.org/engage/) currently describes an enrollment-marketing product, not the involvement platform — a sign of brand confusion we can exploit.

## 4. The "official integration" prize

### 4.1 What a university would ask of us to make us official

| Ask | What it really means | Cost to us |
|---|---|---|
| SSO (SAML/CAS/Shibboleth, InCommon) | Identity is theirs; they can deprovision | Pseudonymous or alumni accounts get hard |
| Registrar/SIS roster sync | Enrollment status gates membership; "currently enrolled students may serve as officers" (UIUC) becomes enforceable | We become an education-record processor |
| FERPA "school official" DPA | "Direct control... with respect to the use and maintenance of education records"; no secondary use; deletion on demand | Kills product analytics on that tenant unless carved out |
| HECVAT + SOC 2 + pen test | Annual security questionnaire ownership | $50–150k/year of compliance overhead |
| WCAG 2.1 AA VPAT | Legal exposure under Title II | Design discipline, ongoing audits |
| Retention analytics dashboard | Involvement × persistence, participation rates by cohort | The actual thing they pay for |
| Hazing / conduct reporting | Per-org conduct history, transparency-report export, incident intake | Moderation and legal-hold tooling |
| Event approval + room booking (EMS/25Live) | Their workflow inserted into ours | Adds friction students hate |
| "See everything" | Admin read access to rosters, events, finances — and, they will ask, messages | The core conflict with student trust |
| Data ownership | The institution owns tenant data and exports on exit | Fine for compliance data; corrosive if applied to chats |

### 4.2 Two models

| | Pure bottom-up (stay GroupMe) | Free for clubs + paid university dashboard |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | Viral, org-by-org; works at community colleges with no portal | Same on the ground, plus institutional endorsement lifts adoption to near-100% at official campuses |
| Revenue | Club-side payments, sponsors, premium features; thin | Subscriptions of the Handshake kind; expandable to hazing/retention modules |
| Trust | Students trust it because the university is not in it | Must ring-fence: compliance data visible to admins, conversations not |
| Legal | FERPA-invisible; but no protection when subpoenaed | FERPA §99.31(a)(9) notification duty runs through the university; data lives inside their governance |
| Risk of ban | Low unless anonymity or money problems | Near zero; but contract terms can force feature removal |
| Precedents | GroupMe (never monetized in higher ed), Discord (Student Hubs faded), RMP (tolerated, ad-funded) | Handshake (university-first, then network effects), Canvas ("freely available under an Affero General Public License" from 2011 while selling hosting, per [Instructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructure)), Slack/Zoom/Notion (bottom-up seats, then IT buys the enterprise tier) |

**Judgment:** the hybrid is the only model that pays, but the order matters. Slack's lesson is that IT bought because the seats were already there and the security team needed control; the enterprise tier sold governance, not features. Our university tier should be sold as *governance over data the university already has a right to* (rosters of record, event approvals, officer designations, hazing history, involvement analytics) — never as visibility into student conversations. The moment we sell chat visibility, we become the next Sidechat hearing.

### 4.3 What we would have to give up

- Serving unrecognized groups *inside an official tenant*. Solution: two-layer architecture — a campus-agnostic student layer (any group, any campus, EDU-verified but not SSO-bound) and an optional institutional tenant that overlays recognition status, approvals and analytics on the groups that opt in. Harvard's failed sanctions show universities cannot even enumerate unrecognized membership; we should not be the tool that finally lets them.
- Free-form payments on custodial campuses (Penn State, Texas A&M, UIUC). Ship ledger/reimbursement modes.
- Our own analytics on tenant data, unless the DPA carves out de-identified product telemetry.
- Speed: every feature touching official data goes through change-management.

## 5. SGA as a channel

Student governments are closer to a purchasing authority than most founders realize:

- **CUNY:** the Board's own bylaws give student government the power to "charter or otherwise authorize teams... organizations... and... refuse, suspend or revoke any charter" (§15.2(b)). At CUNY the SGA *is* the recognizing body.
- **UC Berkeley:** ASUC sponsorship, not university recognition, unlocks funding, office space, "Non-profit Tax ID access, and CalLink financial management"; sponsored orgs are barred from outside bank accounts. The ASUC's [Office of the CTO](https://github.com/asuc-octo) builds and maintains Berkeleytime and Berkeley Mobile — student-fee-funded software that coexists with official systems for years.
- **Georgia Tech:** SGA allocates via annual "Budgets" and rolling "Bills" to RSOs in good standing.
- **UIUC:** SORF (Student Organization Resource Fee) is the funding source only student orgs may tap.
- **Ohio State:** CSA operating and programming funds; **Texas A&M:** SOFAB and the Good Bull Fund; **UCLA:** USAC contingency funding.
- **[ASGA](https://www.asgaonline.com/)** exists precisely to network SGA leaders and advisors nationally and sells services to SGAs.

Because SGAs allocate activity fees and often own the funding-application workflow, an SGA can (a) adopt us as its allocation and reporting tool, (b) fund us from the activity fee as a student service (the Berkeley OCTO precedent), or (c) pass a resolution endorsing us — none of which touches university procurement. The limits: SGA officers turn over annually, SGA contracts above small thresholds still route through the university's fiscal agent, and an SGA endorsement does not confer FERPA "school official" status or SSO. Treat SGA as the way to become *the funding-and-recognition workflow* on a campus, which is the data the activities office most wants, and let the office come to us to buy the dashboard.

## 6. Student privacy and student-run data

### 6.1 Who owns club data

- **In an official portal**, rosters, event submissions and forms are "maintained by... a party acting for the agency" and are education records; the university owns them and governs disclosure. Directory information (name, etc.) can be released unless a student opts out.
- **In a bottom-up tool**, the same data is not an education record. Ownership defaults to our terms of service. Best practice: the *organization* owns organizational records (constitution, event history, treasury ledger) with officer-controlled export; *individuals* own their profile and messages; the university owns nothing until it becomes a tenant, and then owns only the compliance layer it is contractually a party to.
- **Graduation:** UIUC and Stanford require current enrollment to hold office; UT Austin and Berkeley require semester roster refreshes. We should model "alumni member" as a first-class status with reduced permissions instead of deleting people, and let orgs (not universities) decide alumni access — this is also the retention-adjacent feature no incumbent has.

### 6.2 Subpoenas, congressional demands and the 2024–2026 climate

- In January–February 2024 the House Education and Workforce Committee demanded from Harvard "records of all antisemitic incidents since January 2021," disciplinary records, and "social media posts by students and faculty"; Harvard produced 2,516 pages before being subpoenaed on February 15, 2024 ([IHE](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2024/02/15/harvard-subpoenaed-documents-house-antisemitism-inquiry)). Comparable demands followed at Columbia, which suspended its SJP chapter in November 2023 and lost ~$400M in federal funding in March 2025 amid the Khalil detention ([SJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_Justice_in_Palestine); [Khalil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Khalil)). Campus presidents told IHE in November 2024: "If we get subpoenas, we'll have to respond." I could not retrieve the primary reporting on Columbia's early-2025 transfer of disciplinary records to Congress, so I state it only as widely reported.
- FERPA permits disclosure "to comply with a judicial order or lawfully issued subpoena" but requires "a reasonable effort to notify the parent or eligible student... in advance of compliance" — except for sealed federal grand jury subpoenas, law-enforcement subpoenas with nondisclosure orders, and ex parte orders in terrorism investigations ([§99.31(a)(9)](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa)). Those exceptions are exactly the instruments used in the current climate.
- Congressional committees are not bound by FERPA's subpoena procedure in the same way, and universities have chosen to produce student social-media posts to them.

**Implications for our retention and encryption posture:**

1. Data we do not hold cannot be produced. Default message retention of 90–180 days for DMs, with org-controlled retention for organizational records.
2. End-to-end encryption for DMs and small group chats is the single strongest institutional-relations move available: it lets us say "we cannot read it either" to Title IX offices, congressional staff and the university alike, while keeping org records (rosters, events, finances) server-side and exportable. This mirrors the split in Section 4.2.
3. Publish a transparency report and a law-enforcement guidelines page from day one; commit to user notification before compliance wherever not legally barred.
4. Legal hold tooling must exist *for the university tenant* (they will be sued) but be scoped to compliance-layer data.
5. Keep hazing and conduct histories at the organization level, not the individual level, wherever the statute allows — the Hazing Act requires organizational disclosure, not member lists.

## University strategy implications

Opinionated takeaways for the first 12 months: be tolerated, then welcomed, then paid.

1. **Never be anonymous.** Every ban in Section 2 was an anonymity ban. Identity-bound, EDU-verified accounts put us in the GroupMe/RMP "tolerated" bucket from day one and make the UNC-style network block politically impossible to justify.
2. **Never claim affiliation.** No university marks, no "University of X" in generated names, a per-campus "not affiliated" footer, and RSO-compatible naming rules baked into onboarding. This is the cheapest goodwill available and the one thing trademark offices actually police.
3. **Make the official portal's job easier, not obsolete.** Export the roster-of-record, officer/advisor list and event log in the exact shape HornsLink/CalLink/Get Involved want. Position as "the layer students actually use; the portal stays the system of record." An activities director who hears that will not escalate to IT.
4. **Payments must be campus-aware.** Ship three modes — wallet (independent campuses like Ohio State), ledger-and-reimbursement (custodial campuses like Penn State, Texas A&M, UIUC), and read-only — and detect the campus at signup. Penn State's "mobile financial applications" ban is the trap to design around, not fight.
5. **Give advisors a free seat in month one.** Advisors must be employees at Texas A&M and Georgia Tech; they are the first university-side users who will vouch for us, and the audit trail of "advisor saw the event plan" is precisely what risk offices want.
6. **Build the Hazing Transparency Report export before anyone asks.** Stable org identity across officer turnover, an event and incident timeline, and a five-year retention option for conduct-layer data turn a compliance headache into our first paid feature. The first reports were due December 23, 2025; the second update cycle is now.
7. **Encrypt DMs end-to-end and keep org records in the clear.** This split is the whole institutional strategy: universities get governance over what they are entitled to, students get privacy where they need it, and we have a defensible answer to Title IX offices, congressional staff and subpoenas alike.
8. **Finish HECVAT 4.0 Core, a VPAT and SOC 2 Type II by month 9.** Not because anyone will buy yet, but because the first champion who wants to sponsor us internally will be asked for them within a week, and Title II accessibility deadlines are now inside the planning horizon.
9. **Use SGA as the recognition and funding workflow, not as a customer.** Win the funding-application process (SOFAB, SORF, CSA, ASUC, GT SGA budgets and bills) and CUNY-style chartering. That data is what the activities office most wants, and an SGA can adopt us with a vote instead of an RFP.
10. **Sell governance, never visibility.** The university dashboard is co-curricular participation rate, involvement × persistence, event approvals, hazing history and FERPA-clean exports. Refuse chat visibility in writing; it is both the ethical line and the reason students will keep the app installed.
11. **Time the Encoura confusion.** Engage was sold twice in six months and is now marketed by an enrollment company whose own "Engage" page describes email campaigns. Target Engage renewals coming due in 2026–2028 with a "bring your own dashboard" pitch to VPSAs who feel orphaned.
12. **Go to NACA regionals, not NASPA, in year one.** NACA is the only room with students and activities staff together; NASPA is for the year we have a dashboard and a reference customer. Take ASGA seriously as an SGA channel.
13. **Start at community colleges and mid-size publics with no portal.** NOVA runs clubs on a Google Sheet. There is no incumbent to displace and no IT gate to pass; the compliance export in (3) is the whole institutional pitch.
14. **Write the two-layer architecture into the terms of service now.** A campus-agnostic student layer that unrecognized and de-recognized groups can use, plus an opt-in institutional tenant. Harvard could not enumerate final-club members; we should be structurally unable to hand a university a list of who is in a group it has cut off.
15. **Publish the transparency report and law-enforcement guidelines before the first request arrives.** Committing to user notification where legally permitted, and to holding as little as possible, is what turns "student privacy" from a slogan into the reason a Dean of Students prefers us to the free-for-all of GroupMe when the next subpoena lands.
