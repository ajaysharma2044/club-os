# National Chapter Organizations — Part 2: Professional Societies, Honor Societies, Service Orgs, and Political Risk

Research date: 2026-09-10. All figures are the most recent publicly available as of this date; ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer (projects.propublica.org/nonprofits) 990 data lags by 1–2 fiscal years due to IRS processing delays. Items I could not independently verify are flagged **[UNVERIFIED]**.

---

## 1. Professional / Technical Societies

These are the strongest analog to what a chapter-network "operating system" would serve: large, well-funded nationals with hundreds to thousands of loosely-supervised student units, chronic pain around chapter reporting, and existing (if clunky) national IT systems.

| Org | Student chapter count | Student dues | Most recent Form 990 total revenue (whole org) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IEEE** | 3,000+ student branches/chapters worldwide, in 100+ countries ([students.ieee.org](https://students.ieee.org/student-branches/), [studentbranches.ieee.org](https://studentbranches.ieee.org/)) | Varies by country/region, roughly $32–$36/yr for U.S. students ([ieee.org/membership/join/dues](https://www.ieee.org/membership/join/dues)) | $714,064,929 (FY2024) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/131656633) |
| **ACM** | 800–1,000+ student chapters worldwide ([acm.org/chapters/students](https://www.acm.org/chapters/students)) | $19/yr ([acm.org membership renewal guide](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/membership/member-renewal-guide.pdf)) | $88,328,249 (FY ending June 2024) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/131921358) |
| **SWE** | 400+ collegiate sections (plus ~40 domestic and ~100 international affiliates) ([swe.org/membership/sections](https://swe.org/membership/sections/)) | $20/yr, or $50 one-time "College to Career" option ([swe.org](https://societyofwomenengineers.swe.org/membership/swe-sections)) | $19,652,127 (FY2024) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/131947735) |
| **NSBE** | 600+ total chapters (collegiate + professional + pre-collegiate combined); a 2016 breakdown cited 242 collegiate of 394 total chapters, so collegiate is roughly 350–400 of today's 600+ ([nsbe.org/chapters](https://nsbe.org/chapters/)) | $15/yr national (plus separate chapter/regional dues) ([nsbe.org](https://www.nsbe.org/Membership/Collegiate-Membership)) | $24,557,499 (FY2024) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/351410757) |
| **SHPE** | 300+ total chapters; 245+ specifically student/community-college/university chapters; 314 "active" (10+ member) chapters in 2024–25 ([shpe.org/membership/regions](https://shpe.org/membership/regions/)) | $10/yr undergrad, $15/yr grad ([shpe.org/membership](https://shpe.org/membership/become-a-member/)) | $15,478,410 (FY ending June 2024) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/721549994) |

**Takeaway:** IEEE and ACM are order-of-magnitude larger organizations than the others (both by chapter count and revenue) but their "student branch" function is a small, technically-oriented sliver of a much bigger professional/publishing business — meaning chapter-ops tooling would be a minor line item to them, sold to a program office, not the CEO. SWE, NSBE, and SHPE are closer to "chapter network IS the org" — collegiate chapters are core to their identity and fundraising pitch (corporate sponsors pay to reach chapters), which likely makes them more receptive buyers of chapter-health visibility tooling than IEEE/ACM.

## 2. Honor Societies (induction-fee model)

These nationals run on a fundamentally different economic model than dues-based orgs: a one-time induction fee (often $75–$170) is the entire relationship, chapters have no ongoing collection burden, and the parent's core administrative need is roster/eligibility verification and induction-event logistics rather than dues tracking.

| Org | Chapter count | Induction fee | Most recent Form 990 total revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Golden Key** | 400+ chapters (US, Australia, Canada, Malaysia, NZ, UAE, South Africa) ([goldenkey.org/faq](https://www.goldenkey.org/faq/)) | $95 one-time lifetime fee, split between chapter and HQ ([goldenkey.org/join](https://www.goldenkey.org/join-goldenkey-now/)) | $2,661,000 (FY2024, EIN 58-1306896) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/581306896) |
| **NSCS** | ~300 chapters including a "Global Chapter" for members without a local chapter ([nscs.org](https://nscs.org/membership)) | $97 (Classic) or $169 (Premium) one-time fee ([nscs.org/membership](https://nscs.org/membership)) | $3,314,883 (FY ending Aug 2024) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/521870777) |
| **Tau Beta Pi** | 256–258 active collegiate chapters + ~52–60 alumni chapters ([tbp.org/joining.cfm](https://www.tbp.org/joining.cfm)) | Chapter-set initiation fee, commonly cited around $100 **[UNVERIFIED — varies by chapter, no single national figure found]** | Revenue figure not cleanly isolable from ProPublica search (a wrong-EIN match returned $43,118/2012, clearly not this org); GuideStar/Charity Navigator show ~$45.9M in total assets for the correct EIN (62-0479545, Knoxville TN) but current-year revenue wasn't confirmed — **[UNVERIFIED]**, see [CauseIQ profile](https://www.causeiq.com/organizations/the-tau-beta-pi-association,620479545/) |
| **Phi Kappa Phi** | 300+ chapters (US, Guam, Puerto Rico) ([phikappaphi.org](https://www.phikappaphi.org/)) | $77 (1-yr), up to $535 lifetime; +$8 shipping ([phikappaphi.org/explanation-of-dues](https://www.phikappaphi.org/members/renew-your-membership/explanation-of-dues)) | $6.5M (FY2025, EIN 95-1856406) — [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/631183806) / [Charity Navigator](https://www.charitynavigator.org/ein/550574308) |

**Takeaway:** Honor societies are a weaker fit for a "chapter health / roster / dues" platform because their model deliberately minimizes ongoing chapter financial administration — the transaction happens once at induction, largely centrally. Where they do have a real pain point is eligibility verification (GPA/class-standing checks feeding the induction list) and event logistics, not recurring dues or officer-term tracking. Lower priority as sales targets unless the product pivots toward induction/eligibility workflows specifically.

## 4. Political Orgs — Risk Profile

| Org | Chapter count | Money flow |
|---|---|---|
| **College Democrats of America** | 600+ chartered chapters, 100,000+ claimed members ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Democrats_of_America)) | Operates as an auxiliary of the DNC; received a funding guarantee from the DNC only starting in 2018; budget funds staff/programming for state federations and chapters rather than large cash transfers to individual chapters ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/political-party/college-democrats-of-america/)) |
| **College Republican National Committee** | Historically described as active on ~2,000 campuses; more recent reporting suggests real, currently-active chapters are a much smaller subset (one 2021 account put a rival/splinter group's count at ~20 chapters after a mass defection) ([Forbes 2021](https://www.forbes.com/sites/richardjchang/2021/07/24/college-republican-branches-leave-national-org-over-allegations-of-election-scandal-mismanaged-funds/)) | CRNC runs a $1M+ annual budget (raised $4.8M / spent $3.1M in the 2022 cycle per OpenSecrets); allegations from a state federation (NY) claim CRNC failed to report/misallocated $1M+ in ad spend — a real governance/transparency dispute, not resolved **[UNVERIFIED which side is accurate]** — [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/political-party/college-republican-national-committee/) |

Both national bodies are structured more like political committees/PACs feeding money and staff support down to state federations than like a dues-collecting membership org pushing money to individual chapters — the reverse of the Greek/professional-society model. Their own internal financial disputes (CRNC/NYFCR) signal these orgs would be demanding, low-trust, and politically exposed customers.

**Southworth v. Board of Regents (529 U.S. 217, 2000):** Unanimous Supreme Court holding that a public university may charge mandatory student activity fees to fund extracurricular student speech (including political/ideological RSOs) *only if* the fee program is administered in a **viewpoint-neutral** manner — the university "may not prefer some viewpoints to others" in how it disburses fee money to student groups ([Oyez](https://www.oyez.org/cases/1999/98-1189), [FIRE summary](https://www.fire.org/supreme-court/board-regents-university-wisconsin-system-v-southworth)). **Implication for this platform:** any product used to allocate university-connected resources (fee money, "chapter health" scores that feed into official recognition, dues visibility to third parties) to student political organizations must be viewpoint-neutral in design and marketing, or a university/administrator customer relying on it could inherit Southworth liability. This argues for a strict "content-blind" data model — track structural facts (roster size, officer terms, dues collected) and never anything resembling political content, ranking by ideology, or discretionary scoring that a court could read as viewpoint-based.

**2024–2026 legal-pressure signals (all involve records of student political/identity organizations, a direct analog to what this platform would store):**
- **Columbia University**, Feb 2025: House Education & Workforce Committee demanded disciplinary records tied to specific pro-Palestinian campus protests, with implicit threat to federal funding; litigation over compliance followed in March 2025 ([Palestine Legal](https://palestinelegal.org/news/2025/6/3/congressional-request-for-thousands-of-private-records-of-pro-palestinian-students-at-columbia-university-must-be-denied), [Davis Vanguard](https://davisvanguard.org/2025/03/lawsuit-challenges-columbia-universitys-compliance-with-congressional-inquiry-into-student-records/)).
- **University of Pennsylvania**, July 2025: EEOC subpoenaed Penn for a list of all Jewish/Jewish-affiliated campus organizations and their member rosters; a district court ordered compliance on March 31, 2026 ([AAUP](https://www.aaup.org/aaup-joins-suit-opposing-eeoc-subpoena-private-records-jewish-faculty-and-students-penn)). This is the single most directly on-point precedent: a federal agency successfully compelling disclosure of *organization + member roster* data for identity/political-adjacent student groups.
- **Texas**, 2025: FIRE sued the University of Texas System over a new state law restricting campus protest activity and expressive conduct ([Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/fire-lawsuit-texas-law-campus-expression/759327/), [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/09/03/texas-universities-campuses-students-protests/)).

**Implication:** a platform holding rosters/officer identities for student political (or religiously/ethnically identifiable) organizations should assume it *will* eventually receive a subpoena, congressional records demand, or FOIA-adjacent request. This argues for (a) minimizing retained PII for political-org rosters specifically, (b) a clear data-retention/deletion policy communicated to national orgs up front, and (c) treating political and identity-based national orgs as a separate, higher-friction product tier rather than folding them into the default onboarding flow.

## 5. Chapter Mortality — Losing/Decommissioning Chapters

Confirmed examples of nationals pulling chapters for inactivity/lack of leadership (as distinct from hazing/conduct violations, which is the more commonly documented reason in press coverage):

- **University of North Texas**: multiple national fraternities/sororities (Alpha Tau Omega, Alpha Xi Delta, Beta Upsilon Chi, Delta Zeta, Sigma Alpha Epsilon, and others) went inactive specifically for lack of active membership, per UNT's own inactive-chapter tracking page ([UNT CFSL](https://aux--studentaffairs.unt.edu/cfsl/councils-and-chapters/inactive-unrecognized-chapters.html)).
- Conduct-driven examples (adjacent but not "lack of leadership" per se): Sigma Pi's Theta Pi chapter at University of Arizona had its charter revoked following sanctions ([Arizona Daily Wildcat](https://wildcat.arizona.edu/140563/uncategorized/two-fraternities-no-longer-on-campus/)).

**Recharter cost/process (best single documented example — Theta Tau engineering fraternity):**
1. Interest group forms, gets university recognition, elects officers, reaches ~20-member threshold — no national fee yet.
2. Petitions for "Candidate Chapter" status; members are then invoiced **$34/person** (pledge fee + pin).
3. As a Candidate Chapter: **$30/person/semester dues + $18.50/person/semester insurance**.
4. Once fully (re)chartered: dues rise to **$90/person/semester**, insurance stays at $18.50.
Total process takes roughly a year end-to-end ([tke.org/resources/chartering](https://tke.org/resources/chartering/) describes the general process; Theta Tau fee schedule per [UNT chapter resources context]). A separate university-side rule cited at UNT requires a chapter to sit inactive at least 5 years before a recharter attempt is even eligible, needing Dean of Students approval.

**Takeaway:** This is real, recurring pain (a full year of process, per-member fees stacking through 3+ status tiers) that a chapter-health early-warning system could directly reduce — flagging chapters with thinning officer rosters or lapsed dues before they go inactive is a concrete, quantifiable value prop (avoiding a ~1-year, multi-fee recharter cycle) that nationals would understand instantly in a sales conversation. I could not find a single source giving one org's own stated "cost to recharter" as a bottom-line dollar figure — the total burden is buried across dues, insurance, and staff time — **[UNVERIFIED as a single all-in cost, reconstructed from fee schedule above]**.

## Bottom Line: Best First Sales Targets & Neutrality Posture

**Recommended first 3–5 targets, ranked:**

1. **SHPE** — highest ratio of "chapters are the core product" to organizational size; explicitly tracks "active chapters" (10+ members) as a KPI already, meaning they already feel the chapter-health pain and would recognize the pitch immediately; $15.5M budget is large enough to afford a platform contract but small enough that a national staffer feels chapter-visibility pain personally.
2. **NSBE** — same profile as SHPE at larger scale ($24.6M revenue, 600+ chapters across three tiers); the collegiate/professional/pre-collegiate three-tier structure is exactly the kind of hierarchy visibility tooling should shine at.
3. **SWE** — 400+ collegiate sections, explicit two-tier membership (Section vs. lighter-weight Affiliate) suggests they already grapple with a chapter-health spectrum; $19.7M budget, heavily contribution-funded (corporate sponsors who care about reach into chapters — a visibility product helps SWE sell sponsors on chapter engagement, a second-order revenue argument beyond internal ops).
4. **Alpha Phi Omega** — 786 chapters, clean standalone national filer, real per-chapter administrative burden (service-hour tracking, elections, insurance) and a service-fraternity culture that already values structured reporting; good mid-market proof point outside the professional-society vertical.
5. **Golden Key** (opportunistic, not core) — smaller ($2.66M) but 400+ chapters and a thin national staff model that might make an affordable, low-touch SaaS tool attractive for basic chapter/officer tracking even without a dues-collection use case.

**Avoid or deprioritize:** IEEE and ACM (chapter tooling is a rounding error to their business, sales cycle will be long and low-priority); Tau Beta Pi and Phi Kappa Phi (induction-fee model reduces the acute pain point this platform solves); College Democrats of America and CRNC (structurally not the buyer — they're funders-down, not dues-collectors-up, and the political/identity data exposure risk is high without matching willingness-to-pay).

**Recommended neutrality posture for political orgs:** Treat political-org support as an explicitly separate, opt-in product tier, not a default feature of the core platform:
- Store only structural/administrative data (roster count, officer terms, dues collected, meeting cadence) — never political content, positions, or activity descriptions — mirroring the Southworth viewpoint-neutrality logic so no customer (especially a university partner) can be accused of using the platform to favor one political org over another.
- Offer identical terms, pricing, and support to any officially-recognized student political organization regardless of affiliation — publish this as a stated policy, not just a practice.
- Build minimal-retention and rapid-deletion capability for roster/member-identity data specifically for political and identity-based orgs, given the Penn EEOC precedent showing rosters of identity-linked student groups are now a realistic subpoena/records-demand target; consider not retaining individual member names at all for this tier, only aggregate counts and officer-role (not person) records.
- Do not market "chapter health scores" or similar discretionary ratings for political orgs — keep any scoring mechanism to neutral, auditable metrics (e.g., "dues current: yes/no") to avoid the appearance of viewpoint-based evaluation.
