Rule of thumb from these data points: roughly **1 RSO per 40-60 undergrads** at large publics/privates, and closer to 1 per 30 at very club-dense campuses.

**Bottom-up extrapolation (our estimate):**

| Tier | # institutions | Avg RSOs | RSOs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large research/flagship (>20k students) | ~150 | 550 | 82,500 |
| Mid-size 4-year (5k-20k) | ~650 | 180 | 117,000 |
| Small 4-year (<5k) | ~1,800 | 45 | 81,000 |
| Two-year colleges | ~1,300 | 25 | 32,500 |
| **Total US RSOs** | **~3,900** | | **~310,000 (range 250k-400k)** |

**E-board officer seats:** RSOs typically carry 4-7 officer roles (president, VP, treasurer, secretary, events/social, marketing, membership). At 5 per org, that is **~1.5M officer seats**, turning over 50-70% annually, so **~1M newly elected officers every spring** who must be re-onboarded. This is the single most important structural fact for the product: the "admin" of your system churns every 12 months by design.

**Global:** applying a much lower density (1 RSO per 150 students, since club culture is a heavily Anglo-American phenomenon) to 269M tertiary students yields a loose global RSO count of ~1.5-2M, with the UK, Canada, Australia, and India as the most club-dense follow-on markets.

### 1.4 Campus advertising market

There is no clean third-party TAM for "campus advertising" as a category. Triangulation:

- Campus digital OOH pricing: **$75-300 per screen per month, or $8-40+ CPM** ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/answers/ooh-basics/how-much-does-digital-advertising-on-campus-cost)). Total US OOH is ~$7.9B ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-out-of-home-ad-spend-nears-8-billion)); campus is a low-single-digit-percent slice, i.e. roughly $150-300M.
- Colleges themselves spend **$2.2B/yr on advertising** (2019) and **$429-623 per enrolled student per year** on marketing ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/with-competition-up-enrollment-down-colleges-are-spending-billions-on-marketing-and-advertising/)). Universities are a real advertiser class for an intra-campus feed (departments, grad programs, study abroad, dining, housing, athletics).
- Brand budgets targeting 18-24 on social: Meta CPMs averaged **$8.17-8.19**, TikTok **$4.82-6.21**, Snapchat **$8.39 rising to $12.84** in late 2025 (+47% YoY) ([Gupta Media](https://www.guptamedia.com/social-media-ads-cost), [SmartyAds](https://smartyads.com/blog/social-media-ads-cost)). Narrow 18-24 single-metro targeting draws higher CPMs than national broad targeting.

**Our sizing:** the realistically capturable "verified college student, targeted by school/club/interest" ad pool is $0.5-1.5B/yr in the US (brands with explicit college programs: telecom, banks/fintech, CPG sampling, streaming, dating apps, tutoring/AI tools, plus universities advertising to their own students). Fizz's advertiser list (Perplexity, Quizlet, Amazon, Sony Pictures, a dating app) is exactly this buyer set ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/marketers/new-social-media-platform-fizz-gives-brands-a-crash-course-in-marketing-to-college-students/)).

### 1.6 Student affairs / engagement software budget

Market research firms disagree wildly: $1.5-1.9B (2025) ([Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/student-engagement-software-market/), [Insight Partners](https://www.theinsightpartners.com/reports/student-engagement-platform-market)) up to $6.35B with higher ed at $3.71B ([MarkWide](https://markwideresearch.com/global-student-engagement-software-market)). Better grounding: the incumbents (CampusGroups/Ready Education, Modern Campus Presence, Anthology Engage, Campus Labs) sell campus-wide licenses in the **$10k-60k/yr** range to student affairs offices; with ~2,600 four-year campuses the realistic paid "student org management" software pool is **~$50-120M/yr**. Small. This is why we should not lead with a university-pay model; it is a consolation-prize market that PE rolls up (see 2.4).

### 2.1 Handshake: the defining comp (and a warning)

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Revenue trajectory | $36M (2020) → $75M (2021) → $120M (2022) → $180M (2023) → $190M (2024) |
| Valuation / funding | $3.5B (Series F, Jan 2022, $200M led by Coatue); $435M total raised |

| Core business | Declined to ~$150M; CEO said it would only ever be "okay," adding "a few million dollars each quarter or two" |

Sources: [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/handshake/), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/handshake-s-arr-crosses-1b-as-ai-training-revenue-surges), [Upstarts Media](https://www.upstartsmedia.com/p/handshake-refounding-layoff-ai), [Pin pricing guide](https://www.pin.com/blog/handshake-pricing/), [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/handshake), [WorkTech](https://1worktech.com/the-campus-recruiting-platform-youre-depending-on-is-looking-for-something-else-to-be/).

**What investors learned:** (a) university-by-university sales built a real moat (1,600 schools) but capped growth at ~$200M; (b) the durable asset was the **verified graph of 20M students with majors, schools, and skills**, which turned out to be worth more as an AI-labeling supply pool than as a job board; (c) three-sided marketplaces with free student supply need a high-willingness-to-pay demand side, and employers turned out to be cyclical and price-sensitive. The lesson for us: build the graph; the first monetization will not be the last.

### 2.2 Campus social: Fizz, Yik Yak, Sidechat

**Fizz** ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/fizz-raises-undisclosed-funding-at-200m-valuation-cap-as-college-social-app-eyes-global-expansion), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/03/college-social-app-fizz-expands-into-grocery-delivery), [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/marketers/new-social-media-platform-fizz-gives-brands-a-crash-course-in-marketing-to-college-students/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ianshepherd/2025/04/18/gen-zs-underground-social-network-just-went-national-and-its-blowing-up/)):
- Founded 2021 by Stanford dropouts Teddy Solomon and Ashton Cofer; $25M Series B (2023), **$41M total**, latest strategic round at a **$200M valuation cap**.
- 800+ campuses, 1M+ users, user base doubled from fall 2024 to 2025, 27M posts; broke its one-day registration record 12 consecutive days at back-to-school 2025.
- **Campus GTM playbook:** paper slips in buildings, tabling, TikTok, and, critically, **paying clubs per member signed up** ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/marketers/new-social-media-platform-fizz-gives-brands-a-crash-course-in-marketing-to-college-students/)). Ambassador program is formalized ([Fizz Ambassador Program](https://home.joinfizz.com/ambassador-program)).
- **Ad model:** direct-sold native in-feed posts, sponsored polls, "front door" takeovers, push notifications, on-campus activations; deliberately low ad load ("you can't just throw a ton of programmatic ads in"); one dating app got **11.5% CTR** and moved App Store rank ~200 → 40 from a one-day takeover; programmatic planned. No published rate card.
- Also: campus marketplace (2024), Gopuff grocery integration (Sept 2025), hired a TikTok alum to build recommendation engine (Feb 2025).
- Risk realized: UNC system moved to block Fizz/Sidechat/Yik Yak on campus networks in March 2024 over harassment ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/16/anonymous-app-sidechat-picks-up-rival-yik-yak-and-users-arent-happy/)).

**Yik Yak:** $73M raised, **$400M valuation (2014)**, top-10 app, present at thousands of schools; killed by harassment, school bans, and a forced de-anonymization; shut down April 2017 with Square paying ~$1M for engineers; relaunched 2021; acquired by Sidechat March 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/04/28/yik-yak-shuts-down-after-square-paid-1-million-for-its-engineers), [Failory](https://www.failory.com/cemetery/yik-yak)). Investors were burned by a product whose core action (anonymous local posting) had no accruing benefit and no durability past the novelty curve.

**Sidechat:** $10M+ raised (2022, ex-Snap founders); ranks among the top four social apps at Harvard with at least half of undergrads active ([Harvard Magazine](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/undergraduate-column/harvard-sidechat-anonymous-social-media-app), [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/sidechat)). Proves campus-scoped density is achievable fast; does not prove monetization.

### 2.3 Classic campus-launch playbooks

- **Facebook:** launched at Harvard, then expanded school by school; per Andrew Chen, "early campus launches became easier over time as students' friends at different schools began to demand the product" ([Lenny's Newsletter / Cold Start Problem](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network)). The atomic network is "the smallest network that can stand on its own," often "on the order of hundreds of people" and "smaller and more specific than you think."
- **Tinder (Whitney Wolfe, 2012-13):** presented at sorority chapter meetings, had every member install, then walked to the paired fraternity; went from **5,000 to 15,000 users** on one tour; used FOMO ("the other house already signed up") ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/1-billion-matches-later-tinder-can-trace-its-to-the-moon/253165), [Scott Clary case study](https://medium.com/scott-d-clary/sorority-parties-to-50-million-users-the-tinder-go-to-market-strategy-marketing-case-study-9c003b48dc8d)). The insight: Greek life is a pre-existing dense graph with meetings, so seeding a *group* installs a network, not a user.
- **Fizz:** same idea, but they pay the club per member. The club *is* the atomic network. This is directly our wedge.

## 3. What VCs look for in campus / vertical social / community products

### 3.1 Network effect type

- **Local (tribal) network effects.** NFX classifies campus networks as tribal: high density of relationships because self-identification drives shared affinities ([NFX Network Effects Manual](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-manual)). Local effects mean value is created campus by campus, so metrics must be reported **per campus cohort**, not blended. a16z's "16 ways to measure network effects" explicitly calls out retention by geography ("oldest markets should retain better than newer markets") and organic share rising over time as the signatures investors will look for ([a16z](https://a16z.com/16-ways-to-measure-network-effects/)).
- **Casey Winters' test:** network effects should let "your core product experience get better faster than the customers you acquire get worse"; for local network effects, CAC should fall and organic share rise market by market ([Casey Accidental](https://caseyaccidental.com/page/2/)).
- **Atomic network:** Chen's minimum viable network is "on the order of hundreds of people" and "smaller and more specific than you think" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network)). For us the atomic network is **one club with an active e-board and ~30+ members using chat and events weekly**, not a campus.

### 3.3 Sarah Tavel's hierarchy of engagement

Level 1: grow users completing the **core action** (for us: "check my club's hub" or "RSVP/pay"). Level 2: retain via **accruing benefits** (involvement history, connections, files, chat history) and **mounting loss** (leaving means losing your record and your club's memory). Level 3: **self-perpetuating** loops (officers recruit members, members found new clubs, employers pull in students). Tavel's "smile graph" (cohorts that re-engage over time) is the artifact to show ([Sarah Tavel](https://sarahtavel.medium.com/the-hierarchy-of-engagement-5803bf4e6cfa), [Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-hierarchy-of-engagement-sarah)). Yik Yak died at Level 1; Handshake reached Level 2 via the verified profile.

### 3.4 Seasonality and the churn cliffs

Three structural cliffs that any campus-product investor will probe:

1. **Summer cliff (May-Aug):** school-related app usage trends lower in summer ([Digital Turbine](https://www.digitalturbine.com/blog/mobile-apps-does-summer-affect-mobile-usage-in-teens)); ChatGPT's own demand dipped visibly in summer 2023 because of students ([OnlineEducation](https://www.onlineeducation.com/features/chatgpt-demand-declines-in-the-summer)). Expect 50-70% MAU declines June-July. Counter: report **term-over-term cohort retention** (Sept vs. Jan vs. next Sept), plan summer product for e-board transitions, recruitment prep, and internships.
2. **Back-to-school spike (late Aug-mid Sept, again January):** late August through mid-September brings a predictable spike in education app searches; seasonal swings settle within 2-3 weeks ([ASO World](https://asoworld.com/en/blog/why-is-your-app-s-ranking-bouncing-around-this-back-to-school-season/)); Fizz broke daily signup records 12 days in a row at back-to-school 2025. All launch capacity should be concentrated in two 6-week windows.
3. **Graduation churn (~25% of users per year, by design):** the honest answer is that graduation is *the* reason campus social products die and LinkedIn-like products survive. Mitigation: (a) the verified involvement record and alumni identity persist after graduation and become the recruiting product; (b) officers hand off to successors inside the product (the *club* is the durable node, not the student); (c) alumni networks have measurable, durable economic value (alumni ties shape where people work after job loss, per [Berkeley L&S](https://ls.berkeley.edu/news/alumni-networks-shape-where-people-live-and-work-after-job-loss-new-economics-research-shows)).

### 3.5 Campus launch playbook (what the best operators did)

- **Seed groups, not users** (Tinder sororities, Fizz clubs). Pay or reward the *org* for full-roster adoption.
- **One campus at a time to density,** then adjacent campuses via friend demand (Facebook).
- **Ambassadors as a paid, structured program** with clear quotas; Fizz's program is public ([Fizz Ambassador Program](https://home.joinfizz.com/ambassador-program)).
- **Two launch windows per year;** everything else is prep.
- **Report per-campus cohorts** (a16z: retention by geography), and show organic share climbing on older campuses.

### 4.2 Two-sided marketplace with free supply

Our supply side (clubs, officers, students) is free and must remain free; the demand side (advertisers, employers) pays. The classic risks: demand side is cyclical (Handshake's job postings -15%), and the supply side will revolt if ad load harms the utility. Fizz's answer (few direct-sold advertisers, native formats, no programmatic yet) is the right starting posture and is what investors will expect to hear.

### 4.3 Which model VCs would back in 2026

The 2026 backdrop: YC's RFS says explicitly to "stop building the next social app" and favors AI-native, services-replacing companies ([YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs), [UrbanGeekz summary](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/05/y-combinator-reveals-15-startup-ideas-it-wants-founders-to-build-in-summer-2026/)). Consumer seed rounds are the smallest of any category at $1.5-2.5M ([Pitchwise](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/median-seed-round-size-by-industry-in-2026-data)). Edtech VC fell to $2.4B in 2024, lowest since 2014, 89% below the 2021 peak, and Q1 2025 was $410M (-35% YoY) ([HolonIQ](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/edtech-funding-drops-again-in-early-2025-fewer-deals-but-bigger-bets), [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/financing-investment/venture-capital-investment-in-global-ed-tech-sinks-to-decade-low/2025/02)).

So the fundable narrative is **not** "campus social network" and **not** "edtech SaaS." It is: **"the system of record for the 310k organizations and ~1.5M officer seats that run student life, which produces a verified involvement graph that no LMS, SIS, or job board has; we monetize the graph's attention (ads) and its signal (recruiting), and we get paid on the money that flows through it (payments)."** That story lets a consumer-social investor see a graph and a B2B investor see a system of record.

## 6. Risks VCs will raise, and the best counterarguments

| Risk | The objection | Counterargument |
|---|---|---|
| **FERPA / privacy** | "You're touching student data; universities will block you." | We are a bottom-up consumer product; students self-register and clubs opt in, so we are not a "school official" handling education records. Directory-type data (name, email, participation in activities) is explicitly FERPA "directory information" ([studentprivacy.ed.gov](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/content/directory-information)). We never ingest grades or SIS data. Where a university *does* want to contract, we sign the standard vendor FERPA addendum ([ED vendor FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/resource_document/file/Vendor%20FAQ.pdf)). Handshake, Fizz, GroupMe, and Discord all run on .edu self-verification without institutional contracts. |
| **University procurement** | "6-18 month sales cycles will kill you." | We do not sell to universities in the first two years. Anthology's collapse shows bundles and top-down don't win; CampusGroups and Presence took a decade to reach 700-1,400 schools and exited to PE. Bottom-up gets us to a campus in one launch window. |
| **Incumbents bundling** (Canvas, CampusGroups, Discord, GroupMe, Instagram) | "Instructure or Discord adds this." | Canvas is course-scoped and faculty-owned; academics pick the LMS, not student affairs (Phil Hill). Discord/GroupMe have no roster, dues, events, or verified identity, and no campus-scoped discovery. Instagram is where clubs *advertise*, not where they *operate*. Fizz's anonymity makes it structurally unable to be a club system of record. The gap is real: no one owns the roster + officers + money + record. |
| **Low willingness to pay** | "Clubs are broke, students are broke." | Correct, which is why they never pay. Money enters via (1) payments already flowing through Venmo/CashApp/Square (dues, tickets), (2) advertisers who already spend on 18-24 at $5-13 CPMs, (3) employers who pay Handshake a median ~$30k/yr for worse signal. |
| **Graduation churn** | "25% of your users leave every year." | The *club* is the durable node; officer succession is a product feature; 1M new officers/yr is a built-in re-acquisition engine. The verified involvement record follows the graduate and becomes the employer-facing product (LinkedIn's durability logic). Alumni retention on a lower-frequency surface is fine; we report term-over-term org retention as the headline. |
| **Ad load hurts the free tool** | "Ads will make students hate it." | Fizz's posture: few advertisers, native formats, no programmatic, measured by CTR (11.5%) not fill. Utility surfaces (chat, roster, payments) stay ad-free; ads live only in the discovery feed, which is opt-in behavior. |
| **Moderation liability** | "Yik Yak. Sidechat. UNC banned them." | We are non-anonymous, roster-scoped, and officer-moderated by design; the club president is the moderator of their own space. Real identity plus institutional affiliation is the single biggest predictor of low abuse. We should still budget for trust-and-safety tooling from day one and say so. |
| **Seasonality** | "Your MAU chart will be a sawtooth." | Every campus business has this (Handshake, Chegg, Fizz). We report fall-to-fall and fall-to-spring cohort retention, and show the summer as our e-board handoff + recruitment prep season with a different core action. |
| **AI disintermediation** (the Chegg objection) | "Why can't an AI agent do this?" | AI can generate an event page; it cannot be the shared roster, the group chat, the ledger, or the verified record. Chegg died because it sold *answers* (content); we sell *coordination and identity* (network). We should be the AI-native version of this workflow, not its victim. |
| **"Handshake already owns the student graph"** | "Handshake has 20M students." | Handshake owns majors and resumes, not involvement; and it has publicly deprioritized campus recruiting (core down to ~$150M, CEO calls it "okay"). That is an opening, not a wall. |

## 7. Metrics a pre-seed / seed deck needs

### Pre-seed ($500K-1.5M on a $8-15M cap, per Carta norms)

- **1-2 campuses at density:** 30%+ of RSOs on the founders' campus with an active officer, 15-25% of undergrads registered.
- **Org-level activity:** 60%+ of onboarded clubs post an event or message weekly during term; 50%+ have 2+ officers active.
- **Student retention:** W4 30%+ (a16z "good"), D30 20%+ during term; DAU/MAU 25%+ term-time.
- **Core action volume:** RSVPs, dues collected, messages per club per week; show a Tavel-style cohort "smile" for at least one campus across two terms.
- **Organic share:** 80%+ of signups from club invites/rosters, not paid.
- **Qualitative:** 20+ officer interviews, 3+ student-affairs offices aware and not hostile.

### Seed ($2-4M on $15-25M post; consumer-social median is $1.5-2.5M, so a "system of record" framing helps)

- **5-15 campuses**, at least 3 launched in a single 6-week window with a repeatable playbook and documented CAC per campus (target <$5k per campus to reach 20% RSO penetration).
- **Term-over-term org retention 70%+** (SMB-SaaS "good" is 60%) and successful officer handoff rate 50%+ across a spring transition.
- **Student W4 40%+, DAU/MAU 35-40% in term**; older campuses retain better than newer (a16z geography test).
- **Payments GMV:** $500k-2M annualized flowing through dues/tickets, take rate tested.
- **Demand-side proof:** 5-10 paid pilots (local businesses, one university department, one campus-recruiting employer) at any price; one advertiser case study with CTR (Fizz's 11.5% is the bar people will quote).
- **Trust & safety:** report rate, response time, zero campus bans.
- **Graph metrics:** verified profiles, involvement records issued, employer pulls.

### Series A (what the seed must set up)

- 50-150 campuses, 500k+ registered students, 25k+ active orgs, 1-2M officer seats represented, $1-3M ARR blended (ads + employer + payments), org retention 75%+, at least one channel (e.g., Greek life or engineering clubs) with 50%+ penetration nationally.

## What this means for us

1. **Size the deck on orgs, not students.** "310k RSOs, ~1.5M officer seats, 1M new officers every year" is a more defensible and more differentiated TAM than "16.2M undergrads," which every campus pitch uses. Bottom-up from per-campus counts (UIUC 800, Cincinnati 600, BU 450) with a 1-per-50-students rule.

2. **The club is the atomic network.** Andrew Chen says atomic networks are "hundreds of people, smaller than you think." One club with a working e-board and 30 members using chat + events weekly is ours. Tinder seeded sororities; Fizz pays clubs per member. We should pay clubs too, but in product value (free dues collection, free ticketing), not cash.

3. **Do not pitch as edtech or as a social app.** Edtech VC is at a 10-year low; YC says stop building social apps. Pitch "system of record for student life + verified involvement graph, AI-native workflows." This makes Reach/Owl/GSV, DRF/Contrary/Pear, and a16z/Goodwater-type consumer funds all able to say yes.

4. **Recommended business model priority:** (1) **Payments** first, because dues/ticketing is a utility that drives adoption, generates GMV data investors trust, and can be a small take-rate floor (Posh raised $22M on exactly this); (2) **Employer / verified involvement record** second, because it is a defensible, non-obvious asset Handshake abandoned and it survives graduation; (3) **Self-serve ads** third, only after 500k+ verified MAU and campus density, sold direct with low ad load like Fizz; (4) **University dashboards** last, opportunistically, never as the core.

5. **Handshake's arc is the whole thesis in one company.** $190M/yr from campus recruiting was "okay"; the $1B+ business came from the verified graph. Our verified involvement graph should be built as an asset from day one (structured roles, tenure, events run, budgets managed), even before we know its highest-value use.

6. **Recommended GTM sequence:** (a) Fall 2026: the founders' campus only, target 30% of RSOs and 20% of undergrads by Thanksgiving; (b) Spring 2027: officer-transition feature ships before elections (this is the retention test); (c) Fall 2027: 5-10 campuses chosen for club density and peer adjacency (Big Ten/ACC flagships and Greek-heavy privates), launched in one 6-week window with paid ambassadors and per-club onboarding; (d) Spring 2028: first employer pilots and first direct-sold advertiser using verified targeting; (e) Fall 2028: 50+ campuses, self-serve ad beta.

7. **Concentrate 80% of growth spend into two windows:** late Aug-mid Sept and the first three weeks of January. Fizz broke registration records 12 days straight at back-to-school; nothing else in the year moves the curve.

8. **Report retention the way a16z and Tavel will read it:** per-campus cohorts, older campuses retaining better than newer, organic share rising, and term-over-term org retention as the headline. Blended MAU will look like a sawtooth and be discounted.

9. **Officer succession is the product's retention feature, not an admin chore.** 50-70% of admins churn every spring. A one-click handoff that preserves roster, ledger, files, and chat is the "mounting loss" that makes clubs stay. Track handoff completion rate as a north-star metric.

10. **Stay non-anonymous, roster-scoped, and officer-moderated.** Yik Yak burned $73M and Sidechat/Fizz got network-blocked by the UNC system. Real identity plus club scoping is both a safety moat and the reason a university will tolerate us without a contract.

11. **Ads: copy Fizz's posture exactly, then out-target them.** Direct-sold, native, few advertisers, CTR as the KPI (11.5% is the bar). Our edge over Fizz is targeting by verified club membership and interest (pre-law society, robotics, Greek chapter) at a $10-25 CPM, above social's $5-13 and inside campus OOH's $8-40 range.

12. **Employer pricing anchor:** Handshake's Talent Engagement Suite median is ~$30k/yr and its postings are down 15%. Price our "verified leadership signal + club-targeted outreach" at $10-25k/yr for a SKU that Handshake cannot replicate; do not try to be a job board.

13. **Never sell student data; sell verified attention and consented signal.** The Frank fraud ($175M, fabricated users) and FERPA sensitivity mean investors will diligence user counts and data handling hard. Keep audited, verified (.edu) counts and a written data policy from month one.

14. **Expect the incumbents to be weaker than they look.** Anthology went bankrupt on the bundling thesis; CampusGroups, Presence, and Symplicity all exited to PE/Constellation at software-services multiples; Canvas is faculty-owned. The realistic competitor is Fizz adding club tools, not Instructure adding a feed. Speed to density on 10 campuses before Fizz turns its club-payment program into a club product is the real race.

15. **Raise sizes:** pre-seed $750K-1.5M at a $10-15M cap from DRF/Contrary/Pear plus angels who built Handshake/Fizz/Partiful; seed $3-4M at $20-25M post after 5-15 campuses at density, led by a consumer fund that has done campus (Kleiner, a16z, Goodwater, FirstMark, Coatue) with an edtech-specialist follow (Reach, Owl, GSV) for the institutional and employer credibility.
