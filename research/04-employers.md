### What employers say they want on the resume

NACE's Job Outlook attribute rankings are stable year to year: problem-solving/critical thinking (~90% of employers rate essential), teamwork (>80%), communication (>75%), with leadership "ranking high in importance but carrying the largest perception gap" between what students think they demonstrate and what employers see ([NACE Job Outlook coverage](https://career.pennwest.edu/blog/2025/10/14/what-are-employers-looking-for-when-reviewing-college-students-resumes-from-nace/), [NACE press on high-impact skills](https://www.naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/the-high-impact-skills-college-students-should-showcase-on-their-resumes)). The Job Outlook 2026 employers' repeated ask: "it is not enough to list skills, employers want to see examples" of situations where the skill was used ([NACE](https://www.naceweb.org/job-market/trends-and-predictions/what-students-need-to-know-about-the-skills-based-hiring-process)). That single sentence is the product thesis for a verified involvement record.

### How employers source college talent

The 2025-26 sourcing stack, in rough order of spend:

1. **Career fairs and on-campus events.** 93.9% of institutions held in-person fairs in 2024-25; median in-person attendance rebounded to ~700 students (from 419 in 2021-22); virtual fairs draw a median of 93 ([NACE via Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/campus-recruiting-strategies/)). Handshake's own cost model: a virtual event costs ~$1,000 and an in-person event upwards of $10,000, with ~$3,491 in staff time and ~$3,017-$5,046 in travel per in-person fair ([Handshake blog](https://joinhandshake.com/blog/employers/costs-career-fairs-events-in-person-virtual-hybrid/)).
2. **Handshake** (18M students, 1,600+ schools, ~750k free-tier employers, ~1,200 paying TES customers) ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/handshake/), [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/handshake-pricing/)).
3. **Symplicity CSM / Recruit** (the career-center system of record at many schools).
4. **LinkedIn Recruiter** and now Hiring Assistant.
5. **RippleMatch** (algorithmic matching, $40-133k contracts).
6. **Yello + WayUp** (CRM + diverse sourcing; ~7M WayUp users).
7. **Affinity-org conventions** (NSBE, SWE, SHPE) as the diversity-recruiting backbone.
8. **Club sponsorships, case competitions, hackathons, campus ambassadors** -- the channel with the best-fit candidates and the worst tooling. Handshake's Campus-to-Career data says a student needs ~8 touchpoints before applying and shows a 2.2x engagement lift after repeated exposure ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/campus-recruiting-strategies/)), which is exactly what club relationships deliver and career fairs do not.

I could not access the NACE 2025 Benchmarks' sources-of-hire table (paywalled, 403). The publicly quoted fact is that 97.9% of employers use career centers; NACE's report explicitly examines "recruiting channels" including student organizations, so buy the $ report ([store](https://www.naceweb.org/store/2025/research-reports/2025-nace-recruiting-benchmarks-report-dashboard/)) before pitching employers -- it is the number they will ask for.

## 3. How companies target specific clubs

**Finance/consulting.** Banks do not "recruit through clubs" in a contractual sense; clubs are the *infrastructure* of target-school recruiting -- mock interviews, alumni speaker access, "direct recruiter introductions," resume drops, and coffee-chat lists ([Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/investment-banking-target-schools-what-they-are-and-why-they-matter), [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-target-schools/)). Recruiters read a school's finance-club event list as a public signal of which banks hire there ([OfferGoblin](https://offergoblin.com/topics/pipeline/investment-banking-target-schools)). At non-targets, club *leadership* is the substitute credential: "start a stock pitch competition... recruit 10-15 members, and run weekly sessions" is explicit advice, and naming matters ("XYZ Investment Group reads better than Business Club") ([Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/investment-banking-target-schools-what-they-are-and-why-they-matter)). Target lists are shrinking (39 -> 25 schools), which makes the club-level signal *more* valuable for everyone outside the list.

**Consulting.** Deloitte, KPMG and others run sponsored case competitions "directly as recruiting pipelines"; judges are consultants and recruiting managers; winners get fast-track interviews ([RoadToOffer](https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/consulting-case-competition-guide), [CaseBasix](https://www.casebasix.com/pages/consulting-case-competitions)).

**Engineering / diversity.** NSBE, SWE and SHPE conventions are the diversity-recruiting backbone for engineering: SWE's WE25 drew 13,930 attendees and gives exhibitors resume-database access and on-site interview booths ([SWE](https://alltogether.swe.org/2025/10/what-you-need-to-know-for-we25/)). Jopwell sold corporate partners "Campus Ambassador programs at target universities," a recruiter portal, and co-hosted events; 51% of candidates who got offers said they would not have applied without Jopwell ([SJF Ventures case study](https://sjfventures.com/case-study/jopwell-works-to-close-recruitment-gaps-and-improve-corporate-diversity/)). WayUp's pitch was that 71% of its 7M-user database was from underrepresented groups ([Yello](https://yello.co/yello-marks-one-year-anniversary-of-wayup-acquisition/)).

**Business fraternities.** AKPsi and DSP chapters sell tiered sponsorships whose top-tier deliverable is a chapter presentation slot and the "brotherhood resume book" ([AKPsi Alabama](https://akpsibama.org/about/corporatesponsorship/)). The national AKPsi organization maintains a corporate partners program and publishes an event-partnership guide ([AKPsi](https://akpsi.org/partners/)).

**Career-center brokered access.** UMBC's Gold tier includes "priority supporting student organization connection requests" and pushing employer content "to targeted student populations" ([UMBC](https://careers.umbc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/221/2024/05/UMBC24-25EmployerPartnershipProgram.pdf)) -- i.e., universities already monetize club introductions, by hand.

**Tech.** Hackathon sponsorship is explicitly priced by recruiting access: the entry tier is literally called "Resume Drop" ($1,000), with higher tiers buying judging seats, tech talks, and API-prize placement ([Hackonomics](https://medium.com/@alexeymk/hackonomics-101-ad619910b134)).

The Zety finding is the most telling: recruiters are retreating to *observed behavior in the wild* because documents are no longer trusted. A club record is observed behavior at scale.

### Skills-based hiring: pronouncements vs. practice

NACE reports ~70% of employers now say they use skills-based hiring for entry-level roles and GPA screening has fallen from 73% to 42% ([Pin/NACE](https://www.pin.com/blog/campus-recruiting-strategies/)). But the Burning Glass Institute / Harvard "Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice" report finds that most firms that dropped degree requirements did not change who they actually hired ([Burning Glass](https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/research/skills-based-hiring-2024)); **[unverified detail]** the widely quoted figures are that only ~37% of firms that dropped requirements made a meaningful change in hiring and the net effect was <1 in 700 hires. Employers say "skills," then screen on proxies. A verified record must therefore be *cheap to consume inside existing screens*, not a new evaluation ritual.

### Verified credential infrastructure

- **Open Badges 3.0** (1EdTech, final June 2024) adopts the W3C Verifiable Credentials data model; badges carry criteria, evidence, and issuer signatures and can be verified without calling the issuer ([1EdTech](https://www.1edtech.org/1edtech-article/new-open-badges-30-standard-provides-enhanced-security-and-mobility/411060)). Credly is certifying as a 3.0 issuer; verifier software is being embedded in job boards and ATSs ([POK](https://www.pok.tech/en/digital-credentials/open-badge-3-0), [Territorium](https://territorium.com/building-trust-opportunity-open-badges-3-0-and-lifejourney/)).
- **CLR 2.0 / LER.** AACRAO + NASPA have run the Comprehensive Learner Record initiative since 2015 (Lumina $1.2M in 2017, Gates $1.2M in 2022); AACRAO says it has supported 50+ CLR/LER projects; CLRs are explicitly meant to capture "co-curricular engagements" ([AACRAO/NASPA](https://www.aacrao.org/programs-initiatives/learning-mobility/innovative-credentials/), [NASPA](https://www.naspa.org/project/comprehensive-learner-record), [Learn & Work Ecosystem Library](https://learnworkecosystemlibrary.com/initiatives/digital-learner-records/)). Tennessee's college system is scaling CLRs statewide ([NASPA press](https://www.naspa.org/press/effort-to-scale-comprehensive-learner-records-the-college-system-of-tennessee-to-implement-competency-based-program)).
- **Reality check.** I found no evidence that employers *request* co-curricular transcripts or CLRs at any meaningful rate; the AACRAO/NASPA literature is supply-side. **[unverified]** Prior AACRAO employer-perception research found employers liked the *idea* but wanted a one-page, standardized, skills-mapped summary rather than an institutional transcript. A decade of CLR pilots without employer pull is the cautionary tale: the record must arrive *inside* the recruiter's workflow (ATS, LinkedIn, Handshake) or it will not be read.
- **LinkedIn** added "Verified Applicant Spotlight" in Feb 2026 to surface identity-verified applicants to recruiters ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-features/)) -- proof that platforms now see *verification* as a paid recruiter feature.

### Does extracurricular leadership predict anything? The evidence

- **Rubin, Bommer & Baldwin (2002), *Human Resource Management* 41(4):441-454.** 618 business students, videotaped assessment center (leaderless group discussions + presentation) rated by trained I/O raters, controlling for Wonderlic IQ, GPA, Big Five, and affect. Club/organization membership predicted better interpersonal skills (MANOVA F(4,596)=5.98, p<.001); fraternity/sorority membership weaker (p<.05); **sports team membership did not**. Being a **club officer** predicted higher communication, decision-making and teamwork (F(4,358)=2.85, p<.05) but not initiative; officer status in Greek life and sports did not. An extracurricular index remained significant for all four skills after controls (betas .09-.16), adding 1-2% incremental R^2 on a base of ~10%. Authors' verdict: recruiter attention to extracurriculars "does not constitute... recruiting malpractice," but effects are modest and self-selection is not ruled out ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/hrm.10053), [PDF](https://www.collegiateassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/acarticle2.pdf)). Cited within: Boone et al. (1988) found 70% of Fortune-800 CEOs held a club office in college; Howard (1986, AT&T) found student government, school paper and debate correlated with managerial effectiveness (r=.16-.20) while athletics did not.
- **651 hiring managers (JABE).** Hiring managers assigned higher perceived employability and *compensation premiums* to candidates with relevant extracurricular/co-curricular involvement; **leadership roles in activities directly related to the target job commanded the highest premiums** ([Journal of Applied Business and Economics](https://articlegateway.com/journals/index.php/JABE/article/view/7942)).
- Biodata is among the best-validated predictors of job performance in the I/O literature (Hunter & Hunter 1984; Schmidt & Hunter 1998 give biodata ~.35 validity, work samples ~.54, structured interviews ~.51; **[unverified]** Sackett et al. 2022 revised these downward but kept biodata and structured interviews near the top). The resume is a bad biodata instrument because it is unstandardized and unverified; a structured, verified involvement record is a *good* one.

Bottom line: the signal is real but modest, is concentrated in **role-relevant club leadership** (not membership, not Greek life, not sports), and is currently measured by the worst possible instrument.

## 5. What recruiters believe about club involvement

- Advice-to-student content universally says leadership positions "are especially valuable on an early-career resume," but the emphasis is on *what you did and can quantify*, not the title ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/extracurricular-activities-on-resume), [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/extracurricular-activities-on-resume-examples)).
- NACE's employer respondents: leadership is "high importance" with the "largest perception gap" -- employers see it far less on resumes than students think they show it ([NACE](https://www.naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/the-high-impact-skills-college-students-should-showcase-on-their-resumes)).
- Recruiters have always inferred leadership and ability from resume extracurriculars (Brown & Campion 1994, cited in Rubin et al.), and recruiters historically rank interpersonal/leadership skills at the top of what they want from graduates.
- The current grievance is inflation: "e-board" titles proliferate, "President" of a three-person club looks identical to President of a 400-member org that runs a $60k budget, and nothing on a resume distinguishes them. I could not pull r/recruiting threads this session (search budget), but the Zety and Resume Genius data above are the quantified version of that grievance: 76% say authenticity is harder to assess, and recruiters are reverting to in-person observation.
- What they wish they could verify, inferred from the evidence: (a) that the role existed and the person held it for the stated dates, (b) scale (members, budget, events, attendees), (c) outcomes (what shipped, what grew), (d) peer-observed behavior (did others choose to work with this person again), (e) relevance to the job family.

## 6. AI in recruiting, 2025-2026

### Handshake's pivot (verified)

- January 2025: Handshake AI launches. $50M revenue in first four months; $100M run-rate in ~8 months ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/inside-handshake-garrett-lord), [AI-Native GTM](https://ainativegtm.substack.com/p/how-handshake-reinvented-itself-for)).
- Late 2025: CEO Garrett Lord declares a "re-founding" around AI and lays off ~100 (15% of 650 US staff); says campus recruiting would remain an "okay" business but the three-sided marketplace is the "golden goose" feeding the AI unit ([Upstarts](https://www.upstartsmedia.com/p/handshake-refounding-layoff-ai)).
- April 2026: ~$1.1B gross annualized revenue (+349% YoY), ~$450M net after contractor payouts; Handshake AI ~$1B gross / ~$300M net; legacy campus recruiting ~$150-190M and softening; 96 SF roles cut; valuation $3.5B ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/handshake/), [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/handshake-s-arr-crosses-1b-as-ai-training-revenue-surges), [WorkTech](https://1worktech.com/the-campus-recruiting-platform-youre-depending-on-is-looking-for-something-else-to-be/)).
- The asset: 500k PhDs and 3M master's holders reachable through the network; experts paid $100-200/hr for reasoning chains, preference rankings and domain evals for frontier labs ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/handshake/), [Lenny](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/inside-handshake-garrett-lord)).
- Acquisitions: Cleanlab (Jan 2026, data quality), Taro (Feb 2026, engineering careers content), Uplimit (Jun 2026, AI skills academy) ([TechBuzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/handshake-acquires-cleanlab-in-talent-grab-for-ai-data-quality)).
- Implication (WorkTech's framing): the dominant campus platform's "strategic priorities have shifted away" from employer-institution matching, leaving "no existing platform [that] combines longitudinal student intelligence for institutions, agentic career guidance for students, and validated outcomes feedback for employers" ([WorkTech](https://1worktech.com/the-campus-recruiting-platform-youre-depending-on-is-looking-for-something-else-to-be/)). Handshake's free tier also got worse in 2025 (DM caps, Talent List removed) ([Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/handshake-pricing/)).

### LinkedIn Hiring Assistant

Announced Oct 2024, GA Sept 2025. Builds a sourcing strategy, runs dozens of searches across the LinkedIn graph, shortlists, drafts outreach. Reported results: 62% fewer profiles reviewed, 48% less time on applications, 69% higher InMail acceptance, 4+ hours saved per role. Agentic hiring products hit a ~$450M annualized run-rate by April 2026. Feb 2026 added AI Applicant Targeting and Verified Applicant Spotlight ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/linkedin-debuts-ai-agent-to-streamline-recruiting-tasks), [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/ai/how-linkedin-built-ai-hiring-agent), [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-features/)).

### What this means for a rich student behavioral dataset

Two very different buyers:

1. **Employers' AI agents** (LinkedIn Hiring Assistant, Handshake TES AI messaging, ATS copilots) increasingly do the top-of-funnel. They consume *structured, machine-readable, verified* fields. A club-involvement graph exposed as Open Badges 3.0 / VCs with evidence links is agent-food; a PDF resume book is not.
2. **AI labs** want expert human judgment (Handshake AI, Mercor, Scale). Undergraduates running clubs are *not* what labs pay $100-200/hr for; PhDs are. The lab-facing value of a club dataset is smaller and ethically fraught (see 7). The defensible play is not "sell student behavior to labs" but (a) an opt-in expert/task marketplace layered on the graph later, and (b) selling *verification* and *targeting* to employers.

Design consequences:

- **Ranking students = an AEDT.** Any score, sort, or "match" shown to an employer that "substantially assists" a decision triggers LL144 bias audits (for NYC roles), Illinois disparate-impact liability, and EU high-risk obligations from Dec 2027. Under Mobley, *we* could be the liable agent, not just the employer. Verified *facts* (held role X, ran N events, managed $Y) are far safer than derived *scores*.
- **Peer signals are the riskiest field.** Endorsements and "would work with again" ratings are precisely the kind of subjective input that encodes in-group bias; if surfaced to employers, they need audit-ready impact-ratio testing by sex and race/ethnicity, and probably should be shown only as aggregated, opt-in, and never as a rank input.
- **FERPA is manageable but shapes architecture.** If the club OS is student-run and the data is created by students/clubs rather than the registrar, it is arguably not an "education record"; if a university adopts the OS as an official system, its data likely *becomes* an education record and disclosure to employers requires either directory-information status (opt-out model) or written student consent (opt-in). Build for **student-held, consent-per-share credentials** (VC model: the student holds the credential and presents it) and you satisfy both regimes and the EU's data-minimization norms.
- **Consent model to adopt:** explicit, granular, revocable, per-employer or per-pool sharing; separate consent for (a) verification of a claim the student already made, (b) inclusion in searchable pools, (c) any AI-lab or research use. Never bundle (c) with (a).

## 8. The "LinkedIn for students" graveyard

| Company | What it was | What happened | Why it stalled |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WayUp** | Student job marketplace + diversity sourcing (~7M users, 71% URG) | Merged into Yello, July 2021; kept as sourcing front-end to Yello CRM ([Yello](https://yello.co/yello-marks-one-year-anniversary-of-wayup-acquisition/), [JMI](https://www.jmi.com/yello-merges-with-wayup-helps-campus-recruiters-fuel-diversity-hiring/)) | Job board economics; self-reported profiles; no proprietary signal beyond demographics |

| **Untapped** | Diversity recruiting platform (filters, events, sourcing) | Acquired by Podium Education, Feb 2024 | DEI-recruiting demand contracted 2023-25; profile data was self-reported |
| **Jopwell** | Community + job matching for Black, Latinx, Native talent; campus ambassadors | Still operating on corporate partnerships; scale never disclosed ([SJF](https://sjfventures.com/case-study/jopwell-works-to-close-recruitment-gaps-and-improve-corporate-diversity/)) | Same demand contraction; community value > data value |
| **Symplicity** | Career-center system of record; employer Recruit product | Alive and entrenched at the institution level; cheap employer tier ($55/post) | Institution-bound; students churn out at graduation; no cross-school graph |
| **Handshake** | Won the category: 18M students, 1,600 schools, ~$190M recruiting revenue | Strategically abandoned the category for AI data labeling in 2025-26 | Recruiting revenue plateaued (~$150-190M) while postings fell 15%; the network's most monetizable asset turned out to be PhDs' judgment, not undergrads' profiles |
| **RippleMatch** | Algorithmic matching, $40-133k contracts | Operating; niche in the largest employers | Matching on self-reported data and school tier; still a resume proxy |

Common failure modes: (1) all of them ran on **self-reported profiles**, so they inherited the resume's trust problem and competed with LinkedIn on LinkedIn's terms; (2) they sold **access to a pool**, which is a commodity once Handshake made posting free; (3) they were **recruiter-first**, so students showed up only during job season and the data went stale; (4) the diversity-sourcing wedge was **policy-sensitive** and contracted hard in 2023-25; (5) none owned the **day-to-day workflow** that generates ground truth.

What is different with actual behavioral data: the club OS is used *weekly* for something students need anyway (running the org), so the record is a byproduct, not a profile students fill out for recruiters. Every fact is attested by a counterparty (the treasurer approved the budget, the advisor signed off on the event, 140 people checked in). That is a different asset class from a profile: it is auditable biodata, which the I/O literature says is one of the few things that actually predicts performance, and it is exactly what Zety's recruiters are trying to observe at bars and gyms.

## Employer product implications

1. **Sell verification and targeting, not a job board.** Handshake made postings free and then walked away; do not compete on postings. The two things employers demonstrably pay five figures for are *access to a specific pool* (RippleMatch $69k avg, Handshake TES $30k median, TAMU Platinum $25k) and *saved recruiter time* (LinkedIn's 62%-fewer-profiles pitch). Position as the layer that makes campus pipelines trustworthy and targetable.

2. **Recommended product: "Club Pipelines" -- an employer subscription to verified, opt-in talent pools defined by club, role, and evidence.** An employer subscribes to, e.g., "treasurers and VPs of finance at 40 finance clubs, with verified budgets >$10k," gets a weekly feed of opt-in students with verified records, and can sponsor, message, or host events through the club's own OS calendar. It replaces the PDF resume book that AKPsi sells for $1,000 and the UMBC "student org connection request" that costs $8,000.

3. **Pricing anchor: between chapter sponsorship and Handshake TES.** Tiers: **Starter $6k/yr** (up to 5 clubs, 1 seat, verification lookups, sponsor listing), **Growth $24k/yr** (25 clubs, 5 seats, pipeline feeds, event co-hosting, ATS export in OB3/VC format), **Enterprise $60-120k/yr** (unlimited clubs, all schools, API, DEI-safe aggregate reporting, dedicated CSM). This slots under RippleMatch and at/above Handshake's median while offering something neither has. Add per-club sponsorship marketplace with a 15-20% take on the $250-$5,000 packages clubs already sell, which gives clubs a reason to keep the ledger clean.

4. **Price on verified-record lookups for the long tail.** Offer any ATS or recruiter a per-lookup verification API ($2-5 per verified involvement record, or bundled) modeled on employment-verification products. Verification is the wedge that gets the data *into* the ATS instead of asking recruiters to visit another site.

5. **The record must be consumable by agents, not just humans.** Publish every attested item as an Open Badges 3.0 / W3C Verifiable Credential with evidence URLs, so LinkedIn Hiring Assistant, ATS copilots, and Handshake's own AI messaging can ingest and check it without a call to our server. The CLR decade failed because employers had to go read a transcript; do not repeat that.

6. **What the verified involvement record must contain** (facts, attested by a counterparty, with dates):
   - Organization identity: name, school, recognition status, size (members, active members), category (finance, consulting, engineering society, business fraternity, hackathon org, ACM chapter, etc.), and *scale tier* so "President" is contextualized.
   - Positions held: title, start/end, elected vs appointed, who attested (predecessor, advisor, e-board vote record).
   - Budget stewardship: total budget managed, funds raised (sponsorships, grants), spend approved, with the approver chain.
   - Events run: count, role (lead/co-lead/volunteer), verified attendance (check-ins), sponsors secured.
   - Projects shipped: deliverables with links (repo, deck, case-competition placement, hackathon result), team size, role.
   - Participation: attendance rate, tenure, continuity across terms.
   - Peer signals: shown only as *aggregated, opt-in, bias-tested* indicators (e.g., "re-selected to lead by peers in 2 consecutive terms"), never as a numeric rating or rank input.
   - Skill tags mapped to NACE's eight competencies and a public framework (so employers' skills-based screens can match), each linked to the evidence that supports it.
   - Provenance metadata: who attested, when, revocation status, and the student's consent scope for this share.

7. **Do not ship a score.** A ranked list or "fit score" turns the product into an AEDT under NYC LL144, a disparate-impact target under Illinois HB 3773, a high-risk system under the EU AI Act (Dec 2027), and a potential "agent" defendant under Mobley v. Workday. Ship filters on verified facts and let the employer's own tooling rank; if a score is ever added, budget for annual third-party impact-ratio audits and public summaries from day one.

8. **Emphasize role-relevant leadership, not involvement volume.** The evidence (Rubin et al. 2002; the 651-manager JABE study) says the payoff is in *officer roles in clubs relevant to the job*; membership, Greek life, and sports carry little signal. Product surfaces and marketing should lead with "verified finance-club treasurer who ran a $40k budget," not "involved in 6 orgs."

9. **Consent is per-share and student-held.** Students hold their credentials and present them; employers see only what a student released to that employer or pool. This satisfies FERPA whether or not the university adopts the OS, keeps us outside "resume database" re-disclosure problems, and makes the EU/Illinois notice obligations tractable. Never bundle employer-sharing consent with any research or AI-training consent.

10. **Go after the channels Handshake abandoned: events, ambassadors, sponsorship.** Handshake's Events/Advocates modules are paid TES add-ons and the company has stopped investing in employer-institution depth. A club OS *owns* the event calendar and the room booking; selling employers a slot in a club's semester (info session, case workshop, hackathon track) with verified attendance and post-event opt-in is a natural $2-10k SKU per event, in line with Handshake's own $1k-$10k per-event cost benchmarks.

11. **Lead with affinity and engineering societies for the diversity use case, but sell it as verification, not demographics.** NSBE/SWE/SHPE chapters are the durable diversity pipeline; employers spend heavily on their conventions. Offer those chapters the same pipeline product, and report to employers on *verified skills and roles*, not protected-class composition, to stay clear of the post-2025 DEI legal climate.

12. **Fix the "e-board inflation" problem explicitly.** Show org scale, budget, and attendance next to every title. This is the single feature recruiters will describe back to you unprompted; it is the difference between a resume line and a record.

13. **Buy the NACE Recruiting Benchmarks report and instrument your own "source of hire" data from day one.** Employers will ask "what is your cost per hire vs. $6,275?" and "what is your conversion vs. the 51% intern rate?" Build the attribution loop (sponsor -> event -> opt-in -> interview -> offer -> hire) so you can answer with your own numbers by the second recruiting season.

14. **Treat AI-lab revenue as a later, opt-in expert marketplace, not a data sale.** Handshake's $1B came from PhDs' judgment at $100-200/hr, not from undergraduate profiles. A club graph could eventually route, e.g., verified ACM officers to paid evaluation tasks, but only as a separate, explicitly consented product. Selling behavioral data itself would poison the student trust that makes the record worth anything.

15. **Target the non-target.** Shrinking target lists (39 -> 25 schools) mean the highest-value users of a verified record are strong students at non-target schools and the employers who cannot afford 25-school on-campus programs. Price and distribute accordingly: free for students and clubs, cheap for mid-market employers who currently pay $329/mo to Symplicity, and premium for the Fortune 500 that already pay RippleMatch $69k for a worse signal.
